The second thing that changes is how the web gets paid for. Most of the internet is paid for by ads. Someone creates the content, drives the traffic, and serves it for free, because advertisers sponsor it. Say I want to learn a new song on guitar. I go to a site that shows me the tabs for free, and down the side it's showing me a pedal, an amp, a cable. My attention pays for the content and the hosting. Now I stop going to the site. I just ask my agent for the chords. It goes to that same site and fetches them, but it doesn't get distracted by the ads, and it doesn't tell me about them. It just gives me the chords. So the ads don't get the eyeballs, the traffic can't be monetized, and the creator is left with the hosting bill.

Those are the two reasons agents need to be able to pay. And that's where x402 comes in. One: an agent can pay for a service without an account or an API key. It just pays for the request, from its wallet. Two: a site can charge per crawl. The same page stays free for a person, and an agent pays a tenth of a cent, or a hundredth, to fetch it. That's not hypothetical. Cloudflare launched pay per crawl in July 2025, and AWS WAF has a Monetize action that answers AI bots with an x402 payment request. Now let's look at trading. This is where I think agents help the most, and it's not about predicting prices. Then you can go further and hand it the trade itself. It used to take a quant dev to build a bot. Now you can describe one in plain English. Pick a basket and let the agent keep it on target. Kinda like your own custom ETF. Forty percent bitcoin, twenty-five SOL, fifteen HYPE, twenty in dollars. Every week the agent checks how far each one has drifted. If SOL ran and HYPE dipped, it trims SOL and tops up the rest, back to target. Funds charge a fee every year to do exactly this. I wonder how many people would rather just describe their own. [click] Then, to build into that basket, my first bot was a dynamic DCA skill. Every week it starts from a base, say fifty dollars, and scales it by the seven-day average over today's price. Price halves, it buys a hundred. Price doubles, it buys twenty-five. And the DCA never stops: the minimum buy stays at twenty, the max at a hundred. The skill's on GitHub if you want it. Now, a reality check. On Polymarket, fourteen of the top twenty traders are bots. But when frontier models were handed real money to trade in Alpha Arena, most of them lost between forty and fifty-eight percent. So agents don't give you a crystal ball. They're cheap, smart employees that never sleep. Which is why the agents I like best are the boring ones: they check a number every day, and they ask before they act.
